# Chapter 1: Set objectives — keep the team on what matters

> **Read when**: Drafting first O/KR, mission vs strategy tension, calibrating challenge. Templates → [examples.md](../examples.md).

## Core Idea
Growth’s first lesson isn’t “do more” — keep the mission stable, adjust strategy when needed, and pin the team with **one challenging objective + a few measurable key results**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Mission vs strategy**: Mission stays (e.g. connect people to great tea); channels, packaging, staffing are strategy. When a deal conflicts with mission, name the conflict before deciding.
  - When: Big deals, new channels, people moves shake “what are we?”
  - How: Write mission → test the biggest move against it → keep mission, change strategy, or refuse.
- **O + KR intro**: One challenging, clear quarterly objective; 3–4 concrete results that prove it.
  - When: Busy team, weak progress signal, scattered bets.
  - How: Ask “what’s the thorniest worthy problem in 90 days?” then “how would we know we solved it?”
- **Challenge calibration**: KRs hard but not impossible; too low (sandbag) and too high (despair) both hurt.
  - When: First numeric KR draft.
  - How: Anchor on ~50% confidence (later formalized as 5/10).

## Key Concepts
- **Objective**: Direction and inspiration — not a bare KPI sentence.
- **Key Results**: Quantify “is the O done?”
- **Retention/repurchase metrics**: Often better “value proof” than price haggling alone.
- **Capability KRs** (e.g. self-serve ordering): Valid when they enable scale.
- **Optimism**: Needed for startups; not a substitute for focus.

## Mental Models
- Prefer **change strategy, keep mission** when short-term revenue fights long-term identity.
- Use **value-proof objectives** when order $ alone drifts customer quality.
- Treat OKRs as a **focus device**, not fundraising décor.

## Anti-patterns
- Pushing a one-sided marketplace (lots of supply, little demand) with more features/packaging.
- Treating a large contract as strategy when it damages positioning.
- Targets too easy or fantasy-hard.

## Worked Example
Story company sets a quarter to prove premium product grows distributor value. KR directions: ambitious repurchase + improve online ordering to cut manual entry. Align “what value means” before writing revenue slogans.

## Key Takeaways
1. Mission guides tradeoffs; strategy can change.
2. Each quarter: what’s the one hard thing worth solving together?
3. O sets direction; KRs prove arrival; numbers should challenge.
4. Capability work can be a KR.
5. Focus beats doing a bit of everything.
