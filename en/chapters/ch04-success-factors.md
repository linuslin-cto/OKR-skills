# Chapter 4: Factors that decide whether goals land

> **Read when**: Diagnosing “why we miss,” formal O/KR rules, confidence index, Monday quadrants. Agenda → [examples.md](../examples.md).

## Core Idea
Misses rarely mean “didn’t want it enough.” They’re missing priority, repetition, a system, time on important work, or quitting after first failure. Pair OKRs with **mission, correct O/KR craft, confidence, and Monday commit / Friday celebrate**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Five factors goals fail**
  1. No priority (everything important = nothing) → one O + ~3 KRs.
  2. Weak communication → repeat until tired of saying it; embed in meetings/mail/projects.
  3. No system → willpower fails; use commit–celebrate–review.
  4. Time not on important work → urgent crowds important; protect Monday OKR time.
  5. Giving up → first failures differ (too easy/hard/forgotten); winners track what works and retry.
- **Mission before OKRs**: Short, memorable, directive; longer horizon than a quarterly O.
- **Three O principles**: ① Direction + inspiration; ② Time-bound (month/quarter, not a year-long strategy); ③ Independently executable by the owning team.
- **KRs measurable, hard not impossible**: Invert from “how do we know?”; default confidence **5/10**.
- **Cascade**: Company → dept (one KR or whole set) → optional personal.
- **Product teams (Cagan note)**: OKRs should live at cross-functional product-team → business impact, not fight functional personal goals.
- **Monday four quadrants + Friday wins**: This week 3–4 tasks | four-week lookahead | confidence | health metrics; ~1/4 time status, rest coordination.

## Key Concepts
- Confidence 1–10 · health metrics · NPS-like quality proxies · punish-failure cultures that force soft targets · **don’t change OKRs mid-quarter**.

## Mental Models
- Single company O when you need memorability.
- Prefer **system + cadence** over willpower.
- Treat **~50% miss expectation** as healthy positioning, not an excuse.
- Finish a bad set; learn next quarter.

## Anti-patterns
- Many simultaneous company goals.
- Set OKRs then ignore until last week.
- Pure finance KPI sentences as Os.
- Monday as interrogation theater.
- Stuffing every task into OKRs.

## Worked Example
Monday questions: “Does this priority list finish the OKRs? Who helps? Does marketing know product’s next move? Is the team spent / is the product risky?” Trust daily choices; spend the meeting on coordination and blockers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose the five factors before blaming “OKRs.”
2. Mission orients; OKRs mark milestones.
3. O inspires + timed + owned; KRs measurable and challenging.
4. 5/10 confidence + weekly updates.
5. Monday commit, Friday celebrate — every week.
