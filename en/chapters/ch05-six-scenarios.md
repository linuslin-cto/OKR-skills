# Chapter 5: Six practical OKR scenarios

> **Read when**: Quarterly meeting / 7-step cascade / dept coaching / MVP card / weekly report / pitfall check. Pasteables → [examples.md](../examples.md); quick ref → [cheatsheet.md](../cheatsheet.md).

## Core Idea
Turn OKR theory into repeatable meetings and docs: quarterly set, coached dept alignment, seven-step rollout, MVP kanban cards, OKR weekly reports, and a pitfall list.

## Frameworks Introduced

### Scenario 1 — Quarterly OKR meeting
Small group; phones/laptops away; short window for company-wide goal ideas; ~4.5h (two blocks + break) or one block when fluent. Leaders bring 1–2 goals + popular staff ideas → sticky wall → merge → **vote to ~3** → brainstorm metrics → cluster → decide *what* to measure before *X=* → calibrate ~50% confidence → 5-minute final check. Prefer mixed KR types (users/revenue/satisfaction) per O.

### Scenario 2 — Support functions ↔ company goals
Coach “we help sales” into measurable outcomes: disambiguate, align definitions, baseline, outcomes not tasks, positive framing (e.g. **tech qualification rate**). Better co-designed than CEO-imposed.

### Scenario 3 — Seven steps
1. All-hands idea collect → 2. Leadership half-day O+KR → 3. Dept sessions → 4. CEO check → 5. Sub-dept cascade → 6. Optional personal OKRs via **1:1** → 7. All-hands why + demo breakdown + last quarter learnings → execute. Finish setup in ~2 weeks. **First time**: one company OKR; or pilot team; or project-level practice.

### Scenario 4 — MVP / kanban OKRs
Before build: what are we trying to achieve? how do we know success/fail? Optionally call O a **hypothesis**. Rank by business contribution; set measure-by dates; review and learn.

### Scenario 5 — OKR weekly report
1. OKRs + confidence (≤3 red, ≥7 green)  
2. Last week priorities + status  
3. Next week ≤3 complete deliverables (not “chat with legal”)  
4. Risks/blockers (ask help, don’t blame)  
5. Notes  

### Scenario 6 — Common pitfalls
Multiple unmemorable goals · cycle too short (week/month) · appraisal metrics as “inspiring Os” · no confidence / no updates · Monday as status theater · failure modes include all-miss, all-hit with no company benefit, or “OKRs” with no positive effect.

## Mental Models
- Converge goals before filling numbers.
- Coach when functions say “important but unmeasurable.”
- Prefer small first practice over company-wide disillusionment.

## Anti-patterns
- Unprepared quarterly meetings that avoid decisions.
- Two revenue KRs only → short-term gambling.
- Personal OKRs by email only.
- Weekly reports that prove busyness.

## Worked Example
Eng “helps sales” becomes a **tech qualification rate** on major prospects (defined $ threshold) aligned with the sales VP — from activity counts to business outcomes.

## Key Takeaways
1. Quarterly set is a designed decision process.
2. Dept OKRs must verifiably contribute to the company O.
3. Seven-step cascade + two-week deadline.
4. MVP cards and weekly reports are daily carriers.
5. Run the pitfall checklist before each quarter.
