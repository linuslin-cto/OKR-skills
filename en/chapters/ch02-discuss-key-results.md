# Chapter 2: Discuss key results — review problems in flight

> **Read when**: Co-creating KRs, cutting non-P1 work, handling bad news. Review “founder-written, team-ignored” OKRs here first.

## Core Idea
OKRs aren’t a founder monologue. Discuss KRs with the team, cut work that doesn’t move them, face bad news, and use priority + deadlines to land focus in daily work.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Co-create OKRs**: Real OKRs are set together — “this is our company.”
  - When: Season 2+, or season 1 hand-down felt passive.
  - How: Share mission/constraints → co-write O → co-write KRs → prioritize → name owners.
- **Cut non-KR work**: If it’s not P1 for the OKR, pause it — even “pretty” design/projects.
  - When: Mid-week task bloat; busy but KRs flat.
  - How: Map each task to a KR; if none, demote or kill.
- **Face bad news + name owners**: Hiding progress hurts focus more than missing a number.
- **“Say too much = say nothing”**: Few messages, repeated — not a laundry list.

## Key Concepts
- KR discussion forums · role fit · deadlines as useful tension · quality as a KR dimension · cut costs before bankruptcy, not after.

## Mental Models
- Use **co-creation** when last quarter’s “here’s your OKR” killed ownership.
- Prefer **few, repeated** messages over long strategy decks.
- Treat **cutting work** as proof of focus, not cruelty.

## Anti-patterns
- Founder-only OKRs then blaming the team.
- Keeping every legacy project “just in case.”
- Avoiding bad news until quarter-end shock.
- Meeting information overload.

## Worked Example
Leadership stops handing down finished OKRs; uses a shared-ownership metaphor (we built this table together). Non-P1 design work is stopped to protect revenue-linked KRs — focus includes hard tradeoffs.

## Key Takeaways
1. Co-creation raises buy-in and quality.
2. Map work to KRs or cut/demote.
3. Surface bad news early; name owners.
4. Communicate short, precise, repeated.
5. Quality, cost, and staffing are part of focus.
